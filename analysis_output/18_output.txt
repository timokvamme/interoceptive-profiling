QUESTION
--------
The SEM's somatic mediator is the z-scored average of SSS-8 and PCS.
Reviewer 1 argues these index different constructs (bodily symptom burden
vs cognitive appraisal of pain). Do the 21 items form ONE factor here?

B3. FORMAL TEST: DOES THE INTERACTION DIFFER BETWEEN PCS AND SSS-8?

Method. The two single-mediator models cannot be compared informally, since
their coefficients come from the same participants and are correlated. Both
mediators are therefore entered in ONE model as parallel mediators with the
same predictor set and correlated residuals, and the equality of the two
interaction coefficients is tested with a Wald test and with a percentile
bootstrap of their difference.

Validity check (the joint model must not change the coefficients it compares):
  b3 for SSS-8: joint -0.064836 vs single-mediator -0.064836, difference = -2.78e-17
  b3 for PCS  : joint -0.086824 vs single-mediator -0.086824, difference = 2.78e-17
  Identical to numerical precision, as expected when the two mediator
  equations share the same right-hand side. The joint model therefore only
  supplies the covariance needed for the difference test; it does not alter
  the quantities being compared.

Both mediators are z-scored, so the two coefficients are in the same metric
(SD of the respective mediator per unit of the product term) and their
difference is interpretable.

PLAIN-LANGUAGE READING
----------------------
Interaction magnitudes: composite -0.094 | SSS-8 -0.068 | PCS -0.092
The larger single-scale interaction is on PCS.
Significant at .05 on its own: composite YES, SSS-8 YES, PCS YES.

BUT the formal test does not license the claim that the effect is
specifically a pain-catastrophizing effect: the PCS and SSS-8 interaction
coefficients do not differ significantly from each other. The honest
statement is that the interaction is estimated with similar magnitude on
both mediators and that this study cannot resolve which one carries it.

Note also that the composite's interaction can exceed BOTH single-scale
interactions: averaging two z-scored mediators cancels part of their
independent error variance, so the composite can be a more reliable
indicator of whatever common variance the interaction acts on. Compare the
three coefficients above against that possibility before concluding that
one scale 'drives' the effect.
